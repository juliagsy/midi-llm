\begin{abstract}
Recent text-to-MIDI systems extend general-purpose LLMs with domain-specific tokenizers, but no work holds the \emph{training recipe} fixed while varying the symbolic representation and measuring downstream effects on \emph{instruction editing}---the task professional workflows actually require. We present \textbf{MIDI-LLM}, a unified pipeline that serializes MIDI through three active representations---REMI+, Anticipatory MIDI Tokens (AMT), and Octuple---into text consumed by the native Llama~3.2~1B BPE tokenizer (no vocabulary extension). All arms share identical LoRA hyperparameters and are evaluated on the MIDI-Instruct benchmark~\cite{goh2026midiinstruct} for edit success, preservation, and joint pass rate, alongside Llama-BPE efficiency metrics that are comparable across arms. Version~0.1 implements Stage~S3 edit adaptation; earlier curriculum stages (syntax pretraining, domain CPT, caption alignment) are specified but deferred. We release shard builders, training and inference code, Colab notebooks, and round-trip fidelity checks. Preliminary hypothesis: representation choice affects editing more than raw sequence length alone predicts; compound Octuple tokens may compress Llama-BPE length without sacrificing edit quality.
\end{abstract}
